# CONCEPT — what this app is, and what it is not

Free Research LLM answers **one decision question** with a researched, machine-checked answer, using only free model and search quotas. It runs on your own computer (macOS or Windows), or on a free cloud server, and you can open it from any device.

**Its one rule:** a model can propose, but only code decides what counts as a result. Grades, delivered links, calculations and "done" messages come from mechanical checks, never from what a model says about itself. The full rule set is in [PROTOCOLS.md](PROTOCOLS.md).

## The flows

| Flow | You do | The app does | What decides the outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ask | Write a question and an expected result, then press **Build the tree** or **Research now** | Splits the question into a tree of pieces (sub-questions) | You review the tree before anything runs |
| Research | Press **▶ Run** | Searches, reads pages, answers each piece, reviews it with a second model | Gates: citations exist, links were seen on fetched pages, numbers are sourced, formulas are recomputed |
| Improve | Press **Improve loop** and set the hours | Tries changes on several pieces at once (one per lane), re-researches each on a copy, then keeps them one at a time | A change is kept only if its piece does not get worse, no regression check breaks, and the measured score of the whole research rises on two measurements by more than the measured noise. The second measurement researches the changed pieces again, so the noise is real; the new best is that second score alone, so one lucky run never raises the bar for later changes. The score counts the share of criteria the best results meet. A kind of change that failed 3 times on 2+ pieces of the same type and was never kept is refused without a test |
| Team | Open the research's share link on another computer and press **⚡ Join** or **Improve loop** while it runs | Adds one lane, so more pieces are researched or tried at once (2 by default, up to 4). The research remembers a joined browser for 24 h after it was last seen: after an app restart or an update, its page reconnects, rejoins by itself, and reloads when the app's build changed, so the other computer needs no restart or click | The same gates; a change that collides with another lane's change is refused and retried |
| Results | Open the **Results** tab | Always shows a computed result: the verdict and a best estimate with a range, even when evidence is thin, marked **PROVISIONAL** with a confidence from the checks. Below it, every unsettled piece by name, tagged **Fix**, **Research further** or **Clarify**, with why and the next step. **Export:** all tables (.xlsx: one sheet per table, exact links as hyperlinks, sortable and filterable columns; opens in Excel, and in Google Sheets through https://sheets.new → File → Import → Upload), report (.md), pieces & actions (.csv), delivered items (.csv), everything (.json), print/PDF | The checks decide the confidence and the action list (`src/research/gaps.mjs`), not the model |
| Talk | Use the chat in the side panel | Answers and proposes changes | Each chat has its own scope (below); every reply passes the reply audit |
| Operate | Press **Terminal**, or a command button | A real shell on the computer that runs the app, plus one-click commands for keys, links, always-on, updates and the quality gate | You type or press every command yourself |

## The screen, left to right

| Left | Middle (tabs) | Right panel |
|---|---|---|
| All researches; pick one | **Decomposition map** (dependency graph) · **Tree & pieces** (tree with checkboxes on the left; table of every piece and the selected piece's report on the right) · **Sources** (pick a piece, see the sources it used) · **Results** · **Report** (memo) · **Gates & logs** (every gate per piece, regression checks, loop trials, event log) | Everything you do: actions on the selected piece (re-research, split, suggest, add, edit, remove, files, instructions) and the chats |

Pieces keep the names of the first version: **Q** is the question, **D1, D2…** are drivers, **D1.1, D1.2…** are sub-questions, shown as `D1.1 · Model price scan` with the role. Each piece's report follows its role: a sub-question shows its research process (method, queries, pages, sources, answer, gates, reviewer, timeline); a driver shows what it rolls up; Q shows the decision, expected result, memo and drivers.

## Lists are researched like a systematic review

When the expected result is a list (50 plots, 10 suppliers), the tree is a funnel: one driver finds candidates per region or source, the others are screening rules applied to each candidate (not averages), and the memo reports how many were identified, screened out (and why) and included. A piece that is asked for listings and returns none is weak, however good its citations.

It runs in this order, and code decides every step (`src/research/brief.mjs`, `src/search/listings.mjs`, `src/research/sweep.mjs`, `src/research/candidates.mjs`):

1. **Concept brief first.** Before any search, code turns your words into a brief: the item kind, the hard price limit, the regions, whether it must be near the sea, and what is excluded (rent, swaps, news, search pages). Countries you name win over group words: "EU" becomes its 27 countries only when you named none of them; "EU" next to "Spain, Portugal" means Spain and Portugal, and the brief says so. The brief goes into every prompt, and it is shown at the top of the candidate table.
2. **Decision model before any research.** For a research type that has one (`src/research/strategy.mjs`; today: low-entry glamping), code splits your question into clauses and maps each clause to a code check with its measure: sea within the e-bike distance from your guidance, the workable months you asked for, the hottest month without air conditioning, room for at least 3 units, a legal path for the buyer, a country price trend above the EU average, and the entry cost. A clause with no check is shown as **not modelled**, never silently covered. Any other list research gets a decision model built for its own question (`src/research/decision.mjs`): three model proposals map clauses to the quantities code can measure (sea, beach or lake distance, workable or comfortable months, hottest or coldest month, things to explore, places to stay, price, area, price trend); a criterion is kept only when a majority propose it, and its limit only when its quote is found in your question or guidance and states that number. Otherwise the limit is taken from an approved case with its source, or the criterion is shown as **asked but no limit stated** and not checked. The item kind is voted the same way and kept only with the words that fetched listing titles use. Every built model is stored as a case (`data/cases.json`); the glamping model is case #1, and similar cases are shown to the models as examples, ranked by how well the runs that used them scored. For glamping, a heat, season or sea limit you state replaces the hand-chosen one. The model goes into the brief, so the tree and every prompt work toward it. The strategy behind it was retrieved online: each rule names its source, and before each sweep the app re-reads every source and records whether its quotes are still there (once a week). A plot counts toward the expected result only when every check is measured and met; tree answers are background, never the verdict.
3. **Off-concept pages are dropped before any model call.** In a list research, a web page that names none of the asked countries (by its address, its language or its text) is dropped and logged, so no model time is spent on it.
4. **Listings are read from the page's own data, the way commercial extraction tools do it:** schema.org JSON-LD, the JSON a site embeds for its own app (for example `__NEXT_DATA__`), and repeated listing cards. Each listing keeps its price, currency, area, place and link. Per site, code learns a **recipe** (which method worked and what listing links look like) and checks it on page 2 once a week. When the site changes, the recipe is learned again (`data/recipes.json`).
5. **Portal sweep, no model calls.** Before the memo, code pages through verified seed portals for each country you named (`src/research/portals.mjs`: Spain pisos.com ≤ €20,000 by coastal region, coastal Turkish provinces on emlakjet, coastal Portuguese districts on imovirtual ≤ €20,000, each read and checked on 2026-10-26) and the portals that already showed listings of the asked kind in the asked regions. Portals are read in an order drawn by Thompson sampling from what each one yielded before: per page read, whether it gave new listings within your limits (`data/arms.json`, shared by all researches, older reads count half after 3 days); the seed list is only a starting prior. Countries are visited in turn, so no named country waits behind another; seed portals are always paged, and a page is kept in reserve for every start still to come. It also uses next-page links, the page count the site publishes, and the site's own price-filter link set to your maximum. The limit is 90 pages per run. A self-improving loop sweeps once when it starts (and after a restart), never in its trials. Code builds only page addresses: from the site's own links or page count, or the verified seed list. Every listing link comes from a page that was read.
6. **Candidate table.** Every listing is screened against the brief: kind, sale (not rent or swap), single listing page, price after currency conversion, and country. The table shows how many were found, kept, left to check (no price shown, or no country shown) and screened out, with the reason for each. Kept rows show the price, area, place, country, whether the sea is stated, and the page each row came from. They are the first items the expected-result check reads, and the memo sees only this shortlist. Export: **Candidate table (.csv)** and **All tables (.xlsx)** on the Results tab. Each row says whether it counts toward the result (fits every check and the external verifier confirmed it), the verifier's verdict, and whether the area matches the area in the listing title.
7. **Site fit, measured per listing.** Code locates each kept listing (coordinates in the listing's data or on its page, otherwise its place looked up in OpenStreetMap's Nominatim inside the listing's own country) and measures, with no model calls: distance to the coastline and the nearest beach (OpenStreetMap), the camping months a year by the Camping Climate Index (Ma 2021, validated on camping occupancy; daily weather of one recent year (2025) from Open-Meteo, fetched once per 0.25° cell and kept on disk (`data/climate-cells.json`), since climate barely changes from year to year and nearby plots share a cell: Humidex thermal comfort from mean temperature and dew point, sunshine hours, and caps for nights under 8 °C, days over 34 °C, over 10 mm rain or wind over 23 km/h; a month with a mean index above 3 is a camping month: acceptable 3–5, good 5–7, optimal 7 or more, Ma 2021 Table 4; an adverse-weather day is set to 3, so a month of only such days does not count), the optimal days a year, and days above 34 °C, things to explore within 15 km (beaches, viewpoints, sights, nature reserves, protected areas, hiking routes), lodging and campsites nearby, the country's house-price trend (Eurostat), zoning words on the listing, and how many pitches the plot holds (Portugal's rural camping park rule: 40 m² a pitch on at most 60% of the land, at most 30 pitches). Inland listings also get the nearest lake or reservoir. Every other listing stays and is ranked by its tier, then the share of decision-model checks it meets, then its entry cost, so a short-season site with a strong location still ranks. The proposal on top of the Results tab ranks inside each group by Pareto layers instead (step 9). Places are cached for 30 days (`data/sites.json`); two measuring workers can share the file, every write merges what is on disk. When a pass leaves rows queued, a second worker keeps measuring them in the background; outside a loop the table is rebuilt when it finishes, inside a loop it is rebuilt between passes and the loop's baseline is re-measured, so a trial is never credited with new measurements. A self-improving loop starts at once with the measurements already stored (it never waits for the map service); the background worker measures the rest, and the loop re-measures its baseline between passes when that finishes. Its trials use only the cache, so a trial sees the same table as its baseline. Other runs measure at most 12 new places and queue the rest for the next run. The hard checks never wait for the public map service: when it is busy (2026-10-27: overpass-api.de answering 502 or after 25–41 s, its mirrors timing out), the sea distance comes from the Natural Earth 1:10m coastline file the app keeps (`data/coastline-ne10m.geojson`, about ±2 km, so a plot within 2 km of the e-bike limit stays "to confirm") and the climate from the stored cells; beaches, sights and places to stay are filled later, at most 40 map queries per loop pass, and a plot that misses a criterion is not called weak while they are pending. Places are measured most promising first: plots whose place is already measured, then located plots that state the sea, then other located plots, then the rest; the table keeps its own order. The expected-result check of a list research counts only rows of this table, and the memo reads the ranked shortlist with its measurements (`src/research/site.mjs`).
8. **Every plot is defended or rejected by the decision model.** Each row gets a tier: **Fits** (every check measured and met), **Trade-off** (a check fails, but something nearby draws guests and makes up for it: a named lake or reservoir within 5 km, 20+ beaches, viewpoints, sights, reserves and trails within 15 km, or the sea within the e-bike ride for a short-season site), **Weak** (a check fails and nothing nearby makes up for it; too little land for 3 units is always weak), **To confirm** (a check could not be measured: no location, no area, no official price series) and **Blocked** (no legal path: a foreign individual cannot hold land in Vietnam; an industrial site). A listing the portal removed (HTTP 404 or 410) is screened out; a missing area is read from the listing page (the largest stated area); a price per m² below median − 2.5 MAD (log scale) for land of the same country and zoning, or among the priced plots within 30 km, makes the row "to confirm"; a listing whose title names a place more than 25 km from its map point is "to confirm" too (a seller can put a Leiria plot on a Lisbon map). The price trend is the Eurostat house-price change deflated by the same year's inflation (HICP), per country, compared with the EU. Place look-ups never use a country name or code as a place, and a match at country, state or province level is refused, so a vague listing is "to confirm" instead of pinned to a wrong point. The memo verdict starts with the table's counts, computed by code and refreshed whenever the table is rebuilt; the model's memo text follows it. Every number the model wrote (key numbers, its verdict, the summary) is then checked against the verified record, the listings that fit every check and that the external verifier confirmed (`src/research/memocheck.mjs`): the verdict says how many match and quotes any that contradict it, and the workbook lists each claim. Each row says why, in numbers: sea and beach distance, season and heat, units the land holds, entry cost (price + transfer tax + 3 bell tents, with what it excludes), price trend against the EU, things to explore and places to stay nearby, the legal path, and the risks to check before buying. The table sorts by any column and filters by tier. Below it, **Outside your countries, worth a look** lists plots that pass every limit except the country (for example Croatia when you asked for Portugal and Spain). They are measured the same way after your own rows, ranked by opportunity (site checks met; the country's real house-price trend over the years Eurostat has, with its 5-year continuation labelled as a continuation, not a forecast; scenic places with 20+ things to explore and at most 2 campsites; entry cost), sortable on their own, exported with the status "outside your countries", and never counted toward your result.
9. **Proposal: business opportunities** (`src/research/proposal.mjs`). On top of the Results tab, code groups every result by the first opportunity it matches: **Fits every criterion** (not refuted by the external verifier, confirmed ones first), **Short-season sea site** (the sea is in reach, the season is shorter than asked), **Nature or lake draw** (a named lake within 5 km, or 20+ beaches, viewpoints, sights, reserves and trails within 15 km), **Lowest entry to start** (cheapest quarter with room for the first units), **Could fit once confirmed** (nothing measured fails), **Other trade-offs**, and **Outside your countries**. Each section says why it is an opportunity. The number in your expected result (for example 50) is a target, not a limit: every result is in the table, and up to 1,000 listings that pass the brief are measured. The candidate table is the list: its first column is the opportunity and the rank inside it, it is sorted by that by default, any column sorts it, and the opportunity buttons above it filter it (each says why it is an opportunity). Inside each opportunity, a result ranks higher only when no other result is at least as good on every measured value (criteria met, entry cost, sea distance, camping months, optimal climate days, things to explore) and better on one: Pareto layers, with no hand-set weights. Select a row: a details panel takes the right column of the screen in place of the chat panel (about a quarter of the width) and shows why the result is proposed, where it stands out in its opportunity ("lowest entry cost of the 16"), every criterion with its measured value, the monthly climate index, the entry cost, what is still to confirm, the risks, the verifier's verdict and how sure it is (confirmed outside the app, measured by the app, partly measured). ‹ › step through the table in its current order, › collapses the panel to a thin "Details" rail and brings the chat back, the rail opens it again, × closes it. On a phone the panel is a bottom sheet over at most 55% of the screen, ▾ collapses it to one line, and the table scrolls clear of it. The workbook's **Proposals** sheet lists every result with its opportunity.

## An external verifier signs off, not the app

The app never certifies its own result. A separate program, `verifier/verify.mjs`, shares no code with the app (a gate fails if it imports anything from `src/`). It runs as its own process whenever the set of plots that fit every check changes, as SpecHarness requires (arXiv 2609.29921: only admissible evidence from qualified providers establishes the result; read-only, fail-closed). It takes its obligations from your own words: the price limit, the countries you named, the e-bike distance in your guidance, the months in your question. For each plot it re-reads the listing page itself (online, price and currency, area, coordinates), reverse-geocodes the country, recomputes the sea distance from the OpenStreetMap coastline and the workable months from Open-Meteo with its own code, and writes a record (`data/verification/<id>.json`, with a SHA-256 of what it saw, plus an append-only `.jsonl`). A plot counts toward the expected result only when every obligation is **confirmed**; **refuted** or **unconfirmed** plots stay visible with the reason and never count. When the record arrives, the table is rebuilt and a running loop re-measures its baseline. Map queries fail over across the public Overpass instances, so one refusing server does not stop measurements.

## Code chooses what a model reads

Every research piece, not only lists, follows one rule: code ranks and filters before any model sees text (`src/research/evidence.mjs`).

- **Evidence passages, not page openings.** Each page is split into sentence-level passages and scored with BM25 against the piece's question, title and metric, as GPT Researcher (its no-key fallback) and PaperQA2 (`evidence_k`) rank context. The model gets the best passages in page order (at most 2,200 characters per source) instead of the first 2,200 characters, which are often menus and cookie notices. Quotes still have to be found in the full page text.
- **Retrieval is graded before the model call, as in Corrective RAG (Yan et al., 2024, arXiv:2401.15884).** If no fetched page has a passage that matches the piece, code searches again with the piece's own question before any model call. Pages that do not match are ranked last, never dropped, because a page in another language ("terreno" for "plot") can still be the answer.
- **One page per site first.** Sources are ordered by relevance, but the best page of each site comes before a second page from the same site, so the model sees independent sources first.
- **Several queries, fused by rank.** A piece's queries are all searched, and a page that several queries find gains score from each (reciprocal rank fusion, k = 60, as RAG-Fusion does), so pages found by more than one wording come first.
- **One page cache.** Research, the portal sweep and the expected-result check read pages through the same cache (6 h, 300 pages). A page is fetched once, and failed reads are not kept.
- **Pages built by JavaScript.** When a page loads (HTTP 2xx) but holds almost no text, because the site builds it in the browser, code renders it once in a headless Chromium (Playwright, `src/search/browser.mjs`): one shared browser, at most 2 pages at once, 25 s per page, closed after 2 minutes idle; `FRL_BROWSER=0` turns it off. A site that refuses automated reads (HTTP 401, 403, 429) is left alone, never retried in a browser.
- **Whole pages.** A page the app reads itself keeps up to 40,000 characters, so a fact deep in a page (after menus and cookie notices) reaches the evidence ranking and the quote checks. Search-API snippets keep 6,000.

## Checks come from your words, not the model's

The model may propose checks for the expected result and for each piece, but code keeps only what your text states: a word that pages must contain only when you ask for it ("must mention X", a quoted word), and counts or price ranges only when you wrote the numbers. "Feasible for a glamping business" describes the purpose; it is not a word every listing must contain. Dropped checks are listed in the research log as "not enforced".

## Request, desired output and regression checks

Every message in a **Piece** or **Research** chat has two parts: the **request** (what you want) and the **desired output** (the result it should reach). The model turns the desired output into machine checks (grade, a number, a value range, linked items, required words), and code stores them on that piece (or on Q) as **regression checks** R1, R2… They are re-checked after every run. A check that passed once and fails later is marked **regressed**. This is reverse engineering: the target comes first, and the research is judged against it.

## The self-improving loop: whole research or selected pieces

Select pieces on the **Decomposition map** (**Select pieces**, then click them) or tick them in **Tree & pieces**, then choose **Improve loop ▾ → Selected pieces…**, or **Entire research…**. A selection sets the loop's focus: the request (Q), the whole primary level (D1, D2…), and along the path down to each selected piece, the pieces that share a parent with it, plus the selected piece's own sub-pieces. The children of other branches are left out. The map highlights the focus. Clicking a highlighted piece (or Q) excludes it and shows it with a dashed red ring; clicking it again brings it back. The loop changes and scores only the focus (grades plus their regression checks). In both modes a change is kept only if the score rises **and no passing regression check anywhere breaks**; otherwise it is reverted (`reverted-regression` in Gates & logs).

## Results that are always computed

The memo never stops at "insufficient data". The model must give a decision and a best estimate, using weak findings as low–base–high ranges and naming the node ids it rests on. Code then grades the result:

- **Provisional** while any piece is not verified or supported. Confidence comes from the checks: the share of passing pieces, and whether the pieces the memo relies on pass.
- **Action plan:** for each unsettled piece, code picks one action. **Fix** means the run errored, a source was invented, or a formula did not recompute. **Clarify** means the piece rests on assumptions or on an input only you can give. **Research further** means no number, unsourced numbers, a reviewer objection, or too few sources.
- **Order:** pieces are ranked by impact × uncertainty × how weak the grade is, a simple stand-in for value-of-information analysis. Pieces the memo cites (★) come first.
- **Buttons:** each row has **Re-research** or **Clarify in chat**.

## The four conversations, and why they never mix

| Chat | Where | It talks about | It can do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Piece** | Side panel, a piece selected | That one sub-question | Edit, split, add or remove pieces, re-research, suggest sub-issues; attached files become sources of that piece |
| **Research** | Side panel, **Research** | The whole tree of one research | The same actions, on any piece of that research |
| **Results** | **Results** tab, or the **Results** chat button | What was delivered and how sure it is | Re-research, split or get suggestions for a weak piece |
| **App** | **App assistant** in the sidebar, or the **App** chat button | The app itself: how it works, keys, other devices, Windows, cloud, terminal, updates, all researches together, and **every change to the app** | Open or stop a research and show device links directly. It only **proposes** a new research, a deletion, a loop, a terminal command or an **app change ticket**: nothing happens until you press the button, and for a change you then choose **Claude Code** or the **free models** |

- A question about the app typed into a research chat is moved to the App chat, answered there, and never stored in the research.
- App reports (app started, keys reloaded, research created, finished, deleted, loop started or ended, resumed after a restart) are written into the App chat. Reports about one research are written into that research's chat.
- No chat can create, delete or start anything long-running by itself. That removes the failure where a chat model "created a research" from a request that was not a research.

## Changing the app from the App chat

Describe what to add, change or improve (features, UI, tests, checks) in the App chat. It answers with an **app change ticket**: the request, a desired output you can check in the running app, and the tests to add. Then you pick the **implementer** with one click:

- **Claude Code** (the primary button, when it is installed on this computer). It uses your Claude plan and is the one that handles real UI and feature work.
- **Free models** (`src/coder/free-coder.mjs`). No Claude credits; weaker, and it needs more attempts for larger changes.

Both work the same way, and neither writes to the live app directly:

1. Code copies the app into a sandbox (`data/work/C1`), so the ticket can only change that copy. Claude Code runs there with its own tools; the free coder has code search the files for the ticket's words, shows it excerpts and takes exact search/replace edits.
2. The sandbox may change `src/`, `tests/`, `e2e/`, `public/` and top-level docs, never the quality gate, the coder, auth, secrets, the terminal or `package.json`. A change may add tests, never remove them; the gate checks the test count before it runs.
3. `npm run quality` runs in the sandbox. On FAIL, the failing tests and assertions go back to the implementer: up to 2 Claude Code runs or 6 free-model turns, and 4 gate runs. If none passes, the app is unchanged and the chat says exactly which gate failed. Every attempt is logged in `data/changes/C1.attempts.jsonl`.
4. On PASS, code backs up the original files (`data/work/C1.backup`), copies the changed files into the app, and restarts it when it runs as the always-on service. **Undo** next to the ticket restores the backup.

Every step is posted in the App chat, including the files changed and what Claude Code cost. **Undo** and **Try again** (with either implementer) are next to every ticket in App changes. A follow-up message — "make the button green instead", "that broke the export", "try again" — becomes the next ticket and starts from the app as it is now.

The model proposes the code; the gate decides whether it reaches the app. `node src/frl.mjs change C1` runs the free coder from the Terminal; `node src/frl.mjs change C1 --claude` hands the ticket to Claude Code there.

While a change runs, the App chat shows what the implementer is doing every 20 seconds (files read, files edited, commands run, what it says). When the change is applied, it lists every changed file with its added and removed lines.

## Every suggestion is ready to execute

Wherever the app tells you to do something, it gives the exact steps: the exact page (URL), button or field name, and command. This covers getting a key, fixing a weak piece, or starting a local model. Key steps for every provider are taken from the provider's own docs and dated (`src/llm/guides.mjs`). They appear on **Models & router** ("How to get it"), on **Setup**, in `node src/frl.mjs keys setup` and `keys set`, and in the App chat. The App chat must copy them instead of saying "go to the website", and its reply audit accepts their links and commands.

## The reply audit (every chat, every reply)

Before a reply is shown, code checks it ([`src/chat/reply-audit.mjs`](src/chat/reply-audit.mjs)):

1. **Links:** every URL must be one the app knows: a page a research fetched, a link in the app's own guides, or this app's own address. Other links are removed.
2. **Secrets:** key and token values, real or invented ("API_KEY=…"), are removed.
3. **Commands:** shell commands must be terminal buttons or appear in the guides.
4. **Placeholders:** "example.com", "your-key", "Your commit message" are flagged.
5. **Claims:** "I have created / deleted / opened / pushed…" is flagged unless that action actually ran.

If a reply fails, the model gets the failures and writes it once more. If it still fails, the bad parts are removed and the message shows **⚠ audit**. A reply that passes shows **✓ audit passed**.

## Files in chats

Every chat takes up to 4 files per message (📎, drag and drop, or paste). Photos, screenshots, PDFs and MP3/WAV audio are read by a free vision model (Gemini, or a free OpenRouter model). CSV, TXT, MD, JSON and HTML are read by code. In a piece chat, the files also become cited sources of that piece. A model's reading of a photo counts as your evidence, not as a checked fact.

## The terminal

The **Terminal** button opens a real shell (zsh on macOS, PowerShell on Windows, bash on Linux) on the computer that runs the app. Every device that opens the app sees the same session, so you can start something on the Mac and watch it from a Windows PC or a phone. The buttons above it replace `start.command` and `keys.command`. They run the same `node src/frl.mjs …` commands on every system: status, links for other devices, restart, always-on, keys (add, check, list, export, import), update from GitHub, quality gate, and Claude Code.

From other devices the terminal is protected by the access token, like the rest of the app. Anyone with your token link controls that computer, so keep the link private.

## What the app is not

- It is not a general chatbot with hidden powers. The App chat knows only the facts and guides it is given, and says so when they do not cover a question.
- It does not write findings from a model's memory. Every finding needs sources the app fetched.
- It does not spend Claude credits. Research, the loop and app changes run on free keys. Claude Code runs only when you start it yourself (**Claude Code** button, or `change C<n> --claude`).
